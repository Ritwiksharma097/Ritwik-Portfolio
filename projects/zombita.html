Zombita

An AI character who administers a live Project Zomboid server. She reads every message, keeps a private read of every player, runs the economy, judges claims, writes the server's news and hunts its cheaters. The model can only propose. Code and the human admins decide what happens to the world.

What it is

Zombita runs State of Undead Purge, my Project Zomboid Build 42 server, as one of its admins. Players know her as a character. They talk to her at her diner in game, in Discord and on the website, and it is the same her everywhere, with the same memory of them.

The goal is a server that runs itself. She gets more control one action at a time, as her record shows it is safe.

So far this season (launched 10 October 2026, after a public beta from 21 September), 399 player accounts have reached the game, and she read 261 active members in the thirty days to mid-November. The server has 24 slots.

How she works

The rule that holds it together: the model proposes, a deterministic engine disposes.

  chat · game events · server logs
        ↓
  PERCEPTION        code: what is worth remembering
                    models: whether to speak
        ↓
  READ + MEMORY     trust · respect · disposition · mood
                    nightly reads, weekly memory rewrite
        ↓
  PROPOSAL          the model can only ask
        ↓
  AUTHORIZE         code checks, an admin confirms,
                    the outcome is logged
        ↓
  the world

Perception

Code reads every message first and decides what is worth remembering, at no model cost. Whether she speaks goes through three steps: a small model screens, a stronger one decides whether and what to answer and which read-only look-ups to make, and a small one writes the words. In the admin channel she works as a fifth admin and investigates with read-only tools before she answers.

Staff calls

When a player asks for help in game chat, she answers them and sends the admins a staff call: who it is, where they said it, their words, what she reads them as, a type (stuck, lost, bug or other) and where they are standing, so an admin can act without asking anything.

A bug report from game chat, 23 November 2026. The player hears back at once, and the admins get the call with her reading of it. Name and position are blurred.

A read of every player

Four hidden gauges, each moving at its own speed. Trust: would she take your word. Respect: have you earned her effort. Disposition: does she like you. And one server-wide mood that colours her voice and nudges prices, refills and fees within set bands. A public reputation with archetypes sits on top, and every player has a knowledge file.

Players see a piece of it on their in-game profile: her take on them and what she thinks of them, written from their own numbers and answers, so no two read alike.

Two players' profiles in the in-game hub. One says his go-to is a baseball bat, and she points out his compound bow has 9,467 kills. The other left the questions blank, so she lets 32,114 kills speak. A name is blurred.

Memory

Notable moments are captured during the day for free. Each night a cheap model writes a dated summary and a stronger one writes a reflection on where the community is. Once a week she folds what she has learned into a new version of her living memory. Every version is kept and can be rolled back, and she reviews her own speak-or-stay-quiet choices in hindsight.

Economy

A finite money supply tied to real players. A pricing pass runs every six hours within set bands, and she makes her own market call once per in-game month. The treasury only refills after three lean months, sized by her mood, and part of any money that sits above the cap for three months is burned. Shopkeepers have their own wallets, deaths cost a funeral fee scaled by how she feels about you, and wealth is taxed weekly.

Judgment

When a player says "I lost my bag", a cheap model gathers the evidence from server logs, chat and stats, and a strong model rules on it with the player's file and trust in view. The verdict goes to an admin.

Running the world

She posts quests and personal jobs, runs Dawn of the Dead horde nights, writes daily lore quiz questions and a twice-weekly illustrated newspaper that admins approve and that arrives in game as an item. She spots alt accounts from how linked accounts actually play, and when a cheater is confirmed she sends a horde after them in game before the kick.

The newspaper is one piece of data drawn in four places: on the website, as a front page posted to Discord, in the Newspaper tab of the in-game hub and in the News app on the phone, and it also arrives as an item players can read. Each issue goes to the admins first to approve, edit or send back. If not enough happened, she skips the issue and says so rather than invent news.

Safety by design

The model never holds the lever. Restarts, item grants and config changes she asks for are proposals. They pass through one authorize function and wait for an admin's confirm, and every one is logged with its outcome. That log is the track record for handing her more.

Routine restarts (the scheduled ones, and the one after a Workshop update) run by code with a full countdown, and a gate in code keeps them out of server events. When something has gone wrong, like the watchdog restarting a healthy server on 10 November, the fix was a rule in code, not a line in a prompt.

New powers start as a dry run. Her Guardian reads the game's console and says what is wrong, what caused it and what it would do, without doing it, until the admins trust its calls. When players send a picture in a ticket she looks at it, describes it and passes it to the admins, because a picture is never proof on its own.

What this project demonstrates

  • An agent with real authority and real limits. She acts on a live world every day, and every action she takes on it goes through code first.
  • Live operations. A full server, a real economy, and the incidents and fixes that come with running one.
  • Systems that stay cheap. Code filters before any model runs, and the heavy work happens in bounded nightly batches.
  • Research. The same system is the subject of my PhD work on handing administrative authority to an AI. phd.ritwiksharma.com
